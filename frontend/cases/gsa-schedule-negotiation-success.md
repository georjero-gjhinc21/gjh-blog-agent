# A Support Assistant That Held Up in Production

## Summary

A regional home-services company was drowning in repeat calls — scheduling, pricing, service-area questions — while its answers lived in a help center nobody updated and ticket notes nobody read. We built a support assistant grounded strictly in their own documents, scoped to three weeks, with the client's team owning it from day one.

## The Challenge

### The Problem
The support team answered the same forty questions all day while complex jobs waited:

- **Repeat volume**: Routine questions consumed most of the phone queue during peak season.
- **Scattered answers**: The help center, the price sheet, and the dispatchers disagreed with each other.
- **No room to experiment**: Leadership wanted AI help but refused a six-month platform project — rightly.

### Root Cause Analysis
The problem was never staffing alone. Answers existed but were spread across three systems with no owner, so every channel gave a slightly different one. An assistant could only help once the underlying answers were fixed and owned.

## Our Approach

### Phase 1: Fix the Answers First (Week 1)
- Audited the forty most-asked questions against the help center, price sheet, and dispatcher practice.
- Rewrote and versioned the correct answers with a named owner for each.
- Deleted or archived everything contradicted or outdated.

### Phase 2: Build Small and Grounded (Weeks 2–3)
- Built the assistant to answer only from the versioned answer set, saying "I don't know — let me connect you" otherwise.
- Ran it with two agents on live calls before widening to the team.
- Tracked every escalation weekly and fixed the underlying answer, not the model.

### Phase 3: Hand Over Fully (Week 4)
- Moved hosting, prompts, and analytics into the client's accounts.
- Trained two staff members to update answers and review escalations.
- Left a one-page runbook instead of a dependency.

## Key Achievements

### Quantitative Results
- **Roughly a third** of routine questions resolved without reaching an agent.
- **Satisfaction steady** through the transition quarter — no dip during rollout.
- **Four weeks** from kickoff to client-owned operation.

### Qualitative Benefits
- Agents spend their days on complex jobs instead of repeating opening hours.
- Every answer now has an owner and a version — useful far beyond the assistant.
- The client team runs the system without us.

## Lessons Learned

1. **Answers before assistant**: Grounding content must be fixed first; the model is the easy part.
2. **"I don't know" is a feature**: A graceful handoff beats a confident wrong answer every time.
3. **Escalations are the roadmap**: Each one points at an answer to fix, not a parameter to tune.
4. **Handover is the deliverable**: If they cannot run it without us, we did not finish.

## Conclusion

This engagement worked because it stayed small until it proved itself: one team, one answer set, four weeks. The assistant earns its place daily now — and the client could fire us tomorrow without losing it. That is how we think every engagement should end.
