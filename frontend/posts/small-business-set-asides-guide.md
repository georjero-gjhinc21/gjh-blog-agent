# What Good Looks Like in an AI Partner

Choosing who builds your AI systems matters more than choosing the model. Models change quarterly; the partner's habits decide whether you end up with something you own and understand, or a dependency wearing a delivery date. Here is what to demand, in order.

## 1. A Small, Paid Start

Never begin with the transformation. Begin with one workflow, a fixed scope, a few weeks — paid, because free assessments are sales documents and everyone knows it. At the end you should hold a written recommendation you can decline: proceed, defer, or stop. If a partner cannot describe an engagement you are free to walk away from, that tells you everything.

## 2. Senior Hands on the Work

Ask who exactly will do the work, by name, and whether that person is in the room now. The common failure is a polished first meeting followed by a handoff to people you have never met. The person explaining the approach should be the person writing the code and answering for it later.

## 3. Ownership in Writing

Code, infrastructure, documentation, and data pipelines should live in your accounts, under your billing, from day one. "We'll hand it over at the end" is how hostages are made. A good partner designs their own exit from the start — they would rather be invited back than be load-bearing.

## 4. The Word No

The most valuable sentence a partner can say is "don't build this." Watch for it. A partner paid by the project who still tells you a job is a pipeline problem, a staffing problem, or simply not worth doing is a partner acting in your interest at their own expense. That is the whole test, and it is the only one that cannot be faked.

## The Unglamorous Costs, Stated Upfront

Honest partners also name the costs nobody puts in the proposal: someone on your side must own the system, headcount growth brings [payroll and back-office load](/partners/gusto), and delivery needs visible [tracking](/partners/clickup) or it drifts. If these surprise you in month four, the partner failed in month one — not at the work, at the telling. Demand the full picture early, in writing, and choose the partner who gives it without flinching.
